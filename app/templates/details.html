What the "signals" really are

GeneGauge is a clean re-implementation of the pattern behind a polygenic risk score (PRS). In a real setting, each signal is the allele count at a single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) for a given person (0, 1, or 2 copies). Effect sizes come from published genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics.

For a demo we keep those details out of the main UI. The math - weight times value, summed across signals - is the same pattern. The labels are deliberately abstract so nothing here can be read as a medical claim.

Score

Score is a weighted sum: score = Σi weighti · valuei, where each valuei ∈ {0, 1, 2}.

Percentile

We simulate a reference population of {{ population_size }} people with independent Binomial(2, p) draws per signal (p itself jittered per signal for realism). The reported percentile is the midpoint of the "strict less than" and "less than or equal" empirical CDFs, which handles ties gracefully.

Bands

Three simple bands: lower than most (< 33rd percentile), typical (33rd - 67th), and higher than most (≥ 67th).

Plugging in real data

The scoring engine is dataset-agnostic. To use a real weights file, format it as signal_id, plain_label, weight, direction_hint and point GENEGAUGE_WEIGHTS_PATH at it. No code changes required.
